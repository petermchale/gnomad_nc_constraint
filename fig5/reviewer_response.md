## Response

**Reviewer:** *A central result is that GC content explains a substantial fraction of the bias observed in the Chen model. Although the statistical association is clearly shown, the mechanistic explanation remains somewhat speculative. In several places the manuscript suggests possible reasons for the bias or notes that the underlying cause is unclear. It would strengthen the study if the authors could further dissect the source of this bias. One potentially informative direction would be to examine the two-step modeling framework underlying Gnocchi ... Leveraging these intermediate outputs could help determine whether the observed biases primarily arise from the triplet mutation model, the regional regression model, or their interaction.*

We agree, and we thank the reviewer for pointing us at the two-step structure: it turned out to be exactly the right place to look. We have carried out the dissection suggested and it now forms a new figure (Figure 5, five panels), a new Results section (L371-381 and L407-441), and a new Supporting Text S1 giving the derivation. The result is no longer a statistical association but a chain of measurements that ends in an intervention: the bias is introduced by the second (regional) step and not by the triplet-context step; within the second step it is carried entirely by non-CpG contexts; its proximate cause is that the regional submodel is fit on a set of sites that differs from the set of windows Gnocchi is scored on, and that differs more and more as GC content rises; and repairing that one mismatch, with nothing else in the pipeline changed, removes the bias. Each step is summarized below with the panel that carries it.

**Which of the two steps introduces the bias (Figure 5A).** Chen et al.'s expected SNV counts are computed in two stages: a context-only stage that assigns each site a mutation probability from its trinucleotide context and CpG methylation level, and a regional stage that multiplies those expectations by a factor fit, per trinucleotide context, to a model of de novo mutation (DNM) probability as a function of regional features including GC content. We were able to ablate the second stage exactly, because the pre-adjustment expected counts are themselves a published intermediate output of the Chen et al. pipeline (`expected_counts_by_context_methyl_genome_1kb.txt`, deposited in the paper's public bucket). We verified genome-wide that this file really is the pre-adjustment quantity by regenerating it from the per-context export written before any regional-adjustment code runs: the `possible` counts agree exactly on all 2,575,299 windows and the expected counts to within 5e-5 relative. Recomputing Gnocchi from those expectations, through the identical z-score and ranking code, gives the blue curve in Figure 5A. Summarizing the bias as the mean of |mean rank - 0.5| over the GC bins of that panel, published Gnocchi scores 0.212 and the context-only ablation 0.093. The GC-dependent bias is therefore introduced by the regional step; the triplet-context step carries less than half of it, and what it does carry has the opposite shape at high GC.

**Which part of the regional step (Figure 5B).** Because the regional stage acts multiplicatively, its contribution to a GC bin can be read off exactly rather than inferred: the multiplier a bin receives is R(g) = sum(E2)/sum(E1) over the bin's windows, and it decomposes as an identity, R_eff = Pi*R_CpG + (1 - Pi)*R_non, where Pi is the share of the bin's context-only expected counts contributed by CpG contexts. Measured this way, the multiplier Gnocchi applies rises from 0.95 to 1.44 across the GC range. The rise is wholly non-CpG: the non-CpG multiplier climbs from 0.95 to 1.78 while the CpG multiplier stays within 2% of one, and the counterfactual in which the non-CpG contexts receive no adjustment is flat to within 0.6% even in the highest GC bin, where CpG contexts carry 43% of the expected counts. We note that a CpG multiplier of one is the correct behavior and not a failure of the regional model: CpG mutability is dominated by methylation, methylation is modeled in the context-only stage, and the regional models are fit per trinucleotide context without a methylation stratification, so there is nothing left for them to adjust. This also answers the reviewer's third possibility, an interaction between the two steps: the interaction is multiplicative and fully accounted for by R_eff, and the decomposition above is an identity holding bin by bin, not a fit.

**Why the regional step goes wrong (Figure 5C, D).** Having localized the bias to the non-CpG regional models, we asked what they are fit on. The DNM submodel is trained on de novo mutations and matched background sites drawn from the whole genome, whereas Gnocchi is scored only on noncoding windows that pass gnomAD's variant-call QC. Those two populations diverge with GC content: the fraction of background training sites lying inside scored windows falls from 0.85 to 0.29 (Figure 5C, upper). More importantly, the excluded territory is not merely absent but different. In windows that fail QC, the empirical non-CpG DNM rate is 1.55 times that of scored noncoding windows through the bulk of the GC distribution and 4.06 times at GC content 0.61, while coding windows track scored noncoding windows to within about 10% across the whole range (Figure 5C, lower). The consequence for the fit is visible directly: restricting the training set to the scored population shrinks the empirical GC dependence of the DNM probability from 2.45-fold and non-monotonic to 1.57-fold and monotonic, which the regional model can then track, instead of missing it by 26% and 29% in opposite directions (Figure 5D). We should be explicit that this stratum is a QC failure and not missing sequence: all 587,902 such windows have their QC inputs on file, and they are absent from the published constraint table because 70.9% of them fall below Chen et al.'s ">= 80% of observed variants PASS" threshold, against only 3.3% failing the 25-35x coverage band.

**The intervention, and two controls (Figure 5E).** Refitting the DNM submodel on the scored population and rescoring the genome, with nothing else in the pipeline altered, reduces the bias statistic from 0.212 to 0.046 - below the 0.093 of the context-only model, so the regional step becomes a net improvement rather than a net harm. Two controls, run through the same code, exclude the obvious alternative explanations: refitting on the full training set reproduces published Gnocchi (0.212), so the effect is not an artifact of our reimplementation of the fitting step; and refitting on a random subsample of the whole genome matched in size to the restricted set also reproduces it (0.210), so it is not a consequence of training on less data. We emphasize that this is an attribution experiment and not a proposed corrected score: it identifies which modeling decision is responsible, and we do not claim that this particular refit is the right way to build a better metric.

**On leveraging the original study's published intermediate outputs.** We took this suggestion up as far as the deposited data allow, and it may be useful to record what is and is not available. The pre-adjustment expected counts are available and are what Figure 5A rests on, as described above; this is the key intermediate output and it makes the ablation a comparison of Chen et al.'s own numbers rather than of two reimplementations. The comparison tables reported in the original study (their Extended Data Fig. 6) cannot answer this question, however: they are a curated variant-classification benchmark of GWAS, fine-mapped and pathogenic variants against allele-frequency-matched negatives, keyed by locus rather than by window, carrying no GC-content column, and scored in z rather than in the residual - the ascertainment alone would confound any GC-dependence read off them. Finally, the code that fits the regional models is not part of the deposited pipeline, which ships the fitted per-context models and the apply-side code only. Everything in Figure 5B-E therefore required us to reimplement that fit; we validated the reimplementation against the deposited fitted models and against the published scores before using it, and Supporting Text S1 documents this, including one discrepancy worth flagging: the published Methods state the adjustment factor as a ratio of linear predictors, whereas the deposited code computes it as a ratio of predicted probabilities. We follow the code, since the code is what produced the published scores.

**Gnocchi versus Depletion Rank.** The reviewer's conjecture is correct, and Figure 5A now makes the comparison directly: removing the regional step from Gnocchi moves it onto the same footing as Depletion Rank, which uses sequence context without a regional adjustment, and its bias becomes comparable. We would add one caution about reading the cross-metric comparison too literally, which is why we lean on the ablation instead: Gnocchi and Depletion Rank differ not only in the regional step but in cohort, window definition and score definition, so the two metrics differing is suggestive, whereas the ablation is controlled - the same variants, the same windows and the same scoring code, with the regional step as the only difference. Both point the same way.

**Guidance for improved metrics.** We think the practical lesson generalizes beyond Gnocchi, and we have said so in the Discussion. The failure is not that the regional features are the wrong features, nor that the regression is the wrong regression; it is that a submodel was fit on one population of sites and applied to another, and the two populations diverge precisely along the axis - GC content - on which the resulting score is most biased. Any constraint metric that layers a learned regional correction on top of a sequence-context model is exposed to the same failure, and the diagnostic is cheap: check that the sites the correction is fit on are the sites it will be applied to, stratified by the features that enter the correction. This complements our existing recommendation that constraint scores be published with a per-interval estimate of model bias.

**What we have deliberately not claimed.** Figure 5D is an in-sample comparison - the retrained submodel is assessed against the DNM labels it was fit on - so we use it only for the shape of the empirical curve and the fit's ability to follow it. The bias claim rests on Figure 5E, which is measured on gnomAD polymorphism counts that the DNM submodel never sees.
