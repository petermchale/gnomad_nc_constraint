Gnocchi's expected count is built in two steps: step 1 predicts it from trinucleotide context and methylation level alone (E1), and step 2 multiplies each context's contribution by a regional adjustment r fit on de novo mutations (DNMs) and matched background sites (E2). R denotes a GC bin's aggregate adjustment, the ratio of summed expected counts Sum(E2)/Sum(E1). 





How Gnocchi's regional adjustment drives its GC bias

Chen et al. predict the expected number of rare variants in a 1 kb window w in two steps. Step 1 uses sequence context and methylation alone. Writing c = (t, m) for a class of possible SNV with trinucleotide context t and methylation level m, n_c(w) for the number of gnomAD-callable sites of class c in w, and p_c for that class's fitted per-site polymorphism probability, E1(w) = Sum_c n_c(w) p_c. Step 2 multiplies each context's contribution by a regional adjustment: E2(w) = Sum_c n_c(w) p_c r_t(c)(w). For each of the 32 trinucleotide contexts an L1-penalised logistic regression is fit to de novo mutations (DNMs) and matched background sites over standardised, PCA-transformed regional features z_t(w), and r_t(w) = sigma(b_t0 + b_t . z_t(w)) / sigma(b_t0), the ratio of the model's predicted probability at w to its predicted probability at the population mean of the features. Two properties of this form are load-bearing below. First, r is a ratio, so an error common to numerator and denominator - a level error in the fitted probability - cancels exactly and never reaches the score; only feature-driven variation survives. Second, r is fit per trinucleotide context only, never per (context, methylation): methylation enters in step 1 alone, through p_c. (The published Methods state r as a ratio of raw logits. The published code computes the ratio of predicted probabilities given here, and the code is what produced the published scores; we confirmed this by loading one of the deposited fitted models and evaluating it at the feature mean, which returns a probability rather than a linear predictor.)

Window set and binning. Every panel of Fig. 5 is computed on the 693,270 putatively neutral noncoding windows of Fig. 1, taken from that window set's own definition rather than re-derived, and used consistently: the panels are evaluated on it, the retrained model of Fig. 5E is fit on training sites restricted to it, and the bottom band of Fig. 5C is membership in it. GC content is the window's own, in the same units as Fig. 2. Windows are binned into 20 equal-width GC bins spanning the window set's range, 0.212 to 0.716. Bins holding fewer than 100 windows, or fewer than 500 training sites in Fig. 5C and D, are not drawn, and the panels are plotted over GC 0.2-0.73 to match Fig. 2A. We also ran the whole figure on a wider reproduction of the same territory built from Chen et al.'s public data alone - noncoding, QC-passing, autosome or PAR, 1,843,559 windows, 2.66-fold more - and every conclusion below holds on both; the numbers quoted are from the narrower set unless stated otherwise.

The rank statistic (Fig. 5A, E). Each window's constraint score is computed from its observed and expected counts with the published pipeline's own formula, chi2 = (O - E)^2 / E and z = -sign(O - E) sqrt(chi2), keeping |z| <= 10, so that high z means constrained. The z score is then converted to a standardised rank within the analysed set, (rank(z) - 1/2) / n, which is uniform on (0, 1) by construction; this is the statistic of Fig. 2A. Each panel plots the mean rank per GC bin, so an unbiased metric sits at 0.5 in every bin and departure from 0.5 is the bias; the summary statistic quoted is the mean of |mean rank - 0.5| over the bins drawn. Because step 1 alone is not scored by the published pipeline, we compute its z from scratch, and we filter and rank every model on the same windows so that the only difference between curves is which expected count they use.

This statistic is sensitive to small multiplicative errors in r, which is what licenses reading Fig. 5B-D as the cause of Fig. 5A. If a window's adjustment is a uniform inflation E2 = f E1, and the observed count is O = E1 (1 + eps), then z2 - z1 = sqrt(E1) [ (f - 1 - eps)/sqrt(f) + eps ], which tends to sqrt(E1) (f - 1) as f -> 1 and eps -> 0. E1 averages 170 per window (median 181), so sqrt(E1) is about 13 and a 10% inflation displaces z by more than one unit, comparable to the spread of z itself. Measured directly by re-ranking the real genome-wide z distribution under a uniform f, the mean rank moves from 0.500 at f = 1 to 0.705 at f = 1.10 and 0.284 at f = 0.90. (Both of those are measured on the wider reproduction, where the whole z distribution is available; they bound the sensitivity rather than reporting a result, and the narrower set is a subset of the same windows.)

Aggregating the adjustment per GC bin, and decomposing it (Fig. 5B). Because r_t(w) carries no methylation index, it factors out of the inner sum and step 2 is a reweighting of the same per-context step-1 counts: E2^t(w) = E1^t(w) r_t(w). A single window therefore receives r_eff(w) = E2(w)/E1(w) = Sum_t omega_t(w) r_t(w), an E1-weighted mean of its per-context adjustments, so two windows with identical r_t still receive different adjustments if their context composition differs - and context composition is what varies with GC. A GC bin receives R_eff(g) = Sum_{w in g} E2(w) / Sum_{w in g} E1(w). Expected counts add, so this ratio of sums is the adjustment the bin actually receives and the weighting is not a separate modelling choice: it falls out of dividing one sum by another. Throughout, lowercase r denotes the per-context, per-window quantity and capital R the bin-level aggregate.

Restricting the same ratio of sums to the four CpG contexts K = {ACG, CCG, GCG, TCG} and to the other 28 gives R_CpG(g) and R_non(g), and writing Pi(g) for the CpG share of the bin's step-1 expected counts yields R_eff = Pi R_CpG + (1 - Pi) R_non. This is an algebraic identity, following only from expected counts adding, and it reproduces R_eff bin by bin to floating point. The counterfactual curve sets r_t = 1 for every non-CpG context and changes nothing else - the fitted CpG models stay, the weights Pi stay - giving Pi R_CpG + (1 - Pi), which is what Gnocchi would apply if it adjusted CpG contexts alone. E1, and its CpG part, come from Chen et al.'s published step-1 expected-count exports. E2 comes from our reimplementation of the regional fit, since the published deposit contains the fitted per-context models and the apply side but no source for the fit itself; we validated it by comparing its aggregate against Chen et al.'s own published post-adjustment expected counts, which it reproduces to 1.7e-5 per GC bin (median 2.1e-6).

The training set and its strata (Fig. 5C). r is fit on DNMs and matched background sites drawn from the whole genome, but applied to, and here evaluated on, the analysed window set. To measure how far apart those two populations drift with GC, each non-CpG training site is mapped to its containing 1 kb tile and assigned to one of four strata: the scored population, defined by membership in the analysed window table rather than by re-deriving its filters; QC-pass putatively nonneutral noncoding windows, which are QC-passing and noncoding but outside that set, having been given up to an enhancer overlap or one of the interval exclusions that define it; QC-pass coding windows, which carry a published Gnocchi score but are set aside by the noncoding restriction; and QC-fail windows, which have no row at all in Chen et al.'s published constraint table. Both training classes are stacked, DNMs and background sites alike: in a case-control design it is the controls that carry the covariate distribution, but the fit minimises its loss over the mixture, so the mixture is the training distribution and it is the mixture that belongs beside the scored population. Stacking the background class alone barely changes the picture, since it outnumbers the DNMs about 12:1.

The QC-fail stratum needs care, because it is not sequence gnomAD failed to cover. Chen et al. kept only windows with at least 1,000 possible variants, at least 80% of observed variants flagged PASS, and mean coverage between 25x and 35x; all 587,902 absent autosomal windows have their QC inputs on file, and the PASS rule dominates the failures (417,097 windows, against 19,396 for the coverage band). We confirmed the filter in the forward direction as well: all 1,984,900 scored windows satisfy all three conditions. Note that the pass_qc flag in the published table is True on every row, so it is inert and a QC failure is visible only as an absent row. The stratum is also a deliberate mixture of coding and noncoding windows - 6.9% of them overlap coding exons, against 7.1% of the QC-passing ones - so QC failure is close to independent of coding status and this is not a coding band in disguise.

The lower row of Fig. 5C compares each excluded stratum's empirical non-CpG DNM rate against the scored population's in the same GC bin. Writing ybar_s(g) for that rate, it plots log[ybar_s(g) / ybar_scored(g)] with error bars of plus or minus one delta-method binomial standard error, sqrt[(1 - ybar_s)/k_s + (1 - ybar_scored)/k_scored], where k is the DNM count in the stratum and bin. The log is the scale on which that standard error is symmetric, and it places a two-fold excess and a two-fold deficit equally far from the reference line. Zero means the excluded sites are exchangeable with the scored ones as far as mutation rate is concerned. Both rows are computed from a single table of per-stratum counts, so they cannot describe different sites: the upper row takes each stratum's site count as a share of its bin, the lower divides its DNM count by its site count.

Estimating P(DNM) two ways (Fig. 5D). Fix a training population and work over its non-CpG sites. The quantity of interest in GC bin g is P_g = Pr(y = 1 | GC in g), the probability that a training site in that bin carries a DNM; by the tower property this equals the expectation over that bin of Pr(y = 1 | x), whose inner conditional is exactly what each trinucleotide context's logistic model estimates. The panel plots one plug-in estimate from each form over the same sites: the empirical proportion ybar(g), the binomial maximum-likelihood estimate, with standard error sqrt[ybar(1 - ybar)/|S_g|]; and the mean of the sites' own fitted probabilities phat(g), evaluated at each site's own feature vector rather than at the window-aggregated values the genome-wide apply uses. The two estimate the same P_g provided the model is calibrated on that bin's covariate distribution, so the difference within a pair is a calibration gap conditioned on GC, and fitted against empirical within a pair is the comparison that carries meaning.

Two cautions govern how the panel may be read. P_g is a probability under the training distribution, not the genome's: every DNM is kept while background sites are subsampled at some rate s, which multiplies the odds by 1/s and shifts logit P_g by -log s - a constant in the features, and in particular in GC. Levels are therefore not comparable across populations, whose case-control ratios differ (12.2 to 13.4 background sites per DNM across the three), while shapes in g are, so each curve is divided by its own site-weighted mean across bins, leaving shape only. And g enters only as a conditioning event: GC content is one of the 13 candidate regional features, but phat(g) averages whatever features the fit actually selected for each context, so a pair can come apart in a bin where GC itself was never selected. Two populations are drawn: the training set as published, and the same set restricted to sites inside the analysed windows, which is the intervention. A third, a control drawn at random from the whole genome and matched to the restricted set in size, separates a better-matched population from simply less data; it lies on top of the original pair, so it is reported under Fig. 5E rather than drawn here.

Refitting the regional adjustment (Fig. 5E). Exactly one thing changes relative to the published pipeline: training sites whose window is outside the analysed set are dropped. Everything downstream is identical - univariate Bonferroni feature selection, standardisation, incremental PCA, L1-penalised logistic regression per trinucleotide context, and the genome-wide apply - so the retrained expected count enters Fig. 5A's statistic through the same joint filter and the same ranking as the other two. Two controls run through the same code establish that the intervention, rather than the reimplementation or the sample size, is what moves the result: a refit on the full training set, and a refit on a size-matched random subsample of it, drawn to have the same number of sites as the restricted set (1,202,413) but the same population as the full one. Both land on published Gnocchi and so are reported numerically rather than plotted. Note that Fig. 5D is in-sample, the retrained model being scored against the labels of the sites it was fit on, whereas Fig. 5E is out of sample, measured on gnomAD polymorphism counts that the DNM model never sees.

Depletion rank (Fig. 5A). Depletion rank is included as an independently constructed comparison, not as a third Gnocchi curve. It is defined on its own overlapping windows - 30.4 million of them over a 3.1 Gb genome after the same enhancer filter applied to the Gnocchi window set - so it is ranked within its own set and binned on its own GC edges, and is never joined to the Gnocchi windows. That comparison is legitimate because the standardised rank is uniform on (0, 1) for every curve, so what is being compared is how each metric's uniform mass redistributes across GC. It is drawn as the complement 1 - DR, so that high means constrained on the same axis as z, and without error bars: its windows overlap, so windows within a bin are not independent and the standard error of the mean would understate the uncertainty by roughly the square root of the ratio of window length to step. The mean curve is unaffected, and the mean is the only thing read off it.

Supporting Figure 7. Chen et al.'s step-1 probability p_c is keyed by methylation level as well as by trinucleotide context, so the large CpG methylation effect is applied in step 1 and is not available for the regional adjustment to apply again. Supporting Fig. 7A quantifies that effect from the published per-class mutation-rate table, normalising each CpG context's C>T probability to its own value at methylation level 0. It also plots, on the same normalisation, the mu column of that table, which despite its name is the same observable read in a 1,000-genome downsample multiplied by one global constant; normalising removes the constant exactly. The gap between the two is saturation, and the model that relates them is infinite sites on a genealogy: if L_n is the total branch length, in generations, of the genealogy of a cohort of n chromosomes, a site of class c with per-generation mutation rate u_c is polymorphic in that cohort with probability 1 - exp(-u_c L_n). The two cohort sizes are therefore two points on one curve, differing only through L_n, and the 76,156-genome end is the flatter of the two. Saturation is not an error: the expected counts built from p_c are compared against observed polymorphism counts, which saturate identically, so it cancels. Supporting Fig. 7B and C are binned over CpG-context training sites and Supporting Fig. 7D over the analysed windows, which is why the two do not end in the same GC bin; Pi in D is the same weight that appears in Fig. 5B's decomposition.
